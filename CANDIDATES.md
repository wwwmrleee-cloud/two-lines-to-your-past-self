# Candidates — Two Lines to Your Past Self

Judge brief (in order): (1) line 1 warns about one specific moment, line 2
says why it mattered; (2) the moment is concrete enough to picture — a time,
a place, a decision, a sentence said out loud; (3) line 2 shows real cause
and effect instead of restating line 1; (4) the two lines work as one, no
wasted words; (5) the voice sounds written to one specific person, plain and
vivid. Gate: 1–280 characters, exactly two lines, UTF-8.

## The eight candidates

### A — the storm road
On the night of the storm, don't drive the coast road home just because the
shortcut saves twenty minutes.
The twenty minutes you save will cost you the car, and the walk back in the
rain is where you finally learn to stop hurrying.

### B — the Tuesday call (WINNER)
When your father calls on Tuesday and asks you to come home, don't say you'll
come next weekend.
Next weekend never comes, and the last thing he ever says to you will be
'take your time.'

### C — the voicemail
Don't delete the voicemail from your grandmother the morning she sings you
happy birthday off-key.
It's the only recording of her voice you'll ever own, and ten years later
you'll play it every birthday just to hear her laugh at the wrong note.

### D — the interview
At the job interview, don't say yes to the salary before you ask who held the
job last and why they left.
The answer is three people in one year, and the raise you never got is the
price of the question you never asked.

### E — the sealed letter
Don't throw away the letter you wrote yourself at seventeen and sealed for
ten years.
When you open it at twenty-seven, the boy who wrote it will tell you exactly
which dream you abandoned, and you'll finally know what to chase.

### F — the exam night
The night before the exam, don't stay up rewriting notes you already know.
Sleep is the only revision that works, and the grade you lose to a 3 a.m.
panic is the one that teaches you to trust what you already learned.

### G — the train platform
Don't tell Mara you love her in a text message on the train platform.
She reads it after the doors close, and the three dots that never turn into a
reply will follow you onto every train for a year.

### H — the bakery flat
Don't sign the lease on the flat above the bakery on March 3rd — the 4 a.m.
ovens will eat your sleep for a year.
You will learn to bake at dawn instead, and the smell of bread will become
the only alarm clock you ever trust.

## Simulated pairwise comparison (judge criteria in order)

- B vs C: both name a concrete moment and a sentence said out loud. B wins
  on criterion 3 — its second line is pure consequence (the missed goodbye
  is the cost of the delay), while C's second line is partly about the
  object (the recording) rather than the moment itself.
- B vs E: E's twist (the letter to a future self) is elegant, but its moment
  is a decision about an object, not a lived scene; B's Tuesday call is a
  scene you can picture, and its last line lands harder.
- B vs A: A's cause and effect is clear, but its moment is a generic storm
  night; B names a person, a day, and a quoted sentence.
- B vs G: G is vivid (three dots, closing doors) but its consequence is a
  feeling that follows you; B's consequence is a fact — next weekend never
  comes, and those were his last words.
- B vs D/F/H: D and F are advice dressed as warnings (a job tip, a study
  tip) with no single lived moment; H's consequence softens into a cozy
  upside, which blunts the warning. B stays a warning all the way through.

## Winner: B

187 characters, 2 lines. A named person, a named day, a decision made out
loud, a quoted last sentence, and a second line that is nothing but cause
and effect: the weekend you promised never arrived, so the words "take your
time" became the last ones you ever heard from him.
